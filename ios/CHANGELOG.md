# Changelog

All notable changes to this project will be documented in this file.

[Unreleased]

### Changed
- Reworked the lose modal in Levels and Seasons. It now shows the spendable star balance beside the lives, so every star price can be read against what pays it. Each rescue's two payments share one row under a title naming what they buy ("Forgive 3 mistakes": [▶ Watch ad] [8 ★]; "Get 1 more life": [▶ Watch ad] [10 ★]; "Add 30 seconds": ad only), and Skip and Menu are text buttons below Try again. The mistakes loss with every option available drops from five full-width buttons to two rows plus a text row. Menu keeps its outlined button when it is the only way out.
- The lose modal no longer shows a life as lost before it is. The loss is only booked when the player leaves it (Try again, Menu or Skip); forgiving mistakes or taking the ad's 30 seconds keeps the life. The modal used to play the heart-break animation on arrival, over the first *already empty* heart (or nothing, with all four lives), while the heart actually at stake still looked safe. That heart now pulses, the rescue says "Continue now and you won't lose a life.", and the heart breaks only when a life is really spent. New `MemorizeViewModel.isLifeAtStake` and `LivesRow.atRiskSlot`.
- Skip this level is no longer offered when skipping would leave no life: when the player is already out of lives, or the skip would spend their last one. Skipping books the loss and returns to the map, whose lives gate then refused to open the level the player had just paid 15 stars to unlock. `canSkipLevelWithStars` enforces the same rule, so the confirm path can't be reached either.
- Removed the now-unused `game_rewarded_extra_time`, `levels_forgive_ad_format` and `levels_forgive_stars_format` strings, and added six `lose_offer_*` / `lose_pay_stars_format` / `lose_life_at_stake` strings in all ten locales.
- Brought Android's lose screen to the same design: star balance beside the lives, one rescue row per loss (Android still has no "+30 seconds" ad, so a timeout offers no rescue there), Skip and Menu as text buttons, the pulsing heart at stake instead of a heart-break on arrival, and Skip withheld when it would leave no life. `GameViewModel.skipLevelWithStars()` now refuses in that case too. New `GameUiState.isLossPending`, `isLifeAtStake` and `livesAfterLoss`, and `LivesRow`'s `atRiskSlot`. The same new strings, except `lose_offer_extra_time`, are added in all ten locales, and `levels_forgive_ad_format` / `levels_forgive_stars_format` are removed. `DOMEMORY_ANDROID_SPEC.md` §7.7 now describes the layout, the life at stake and when Skip is offered.
- Upgraded Tuist from 4.195.6 to 4.208.0 so the project builds on Xcode 27 with no extra step. Xcode 27 rejects iOS deployment targets below 15.0, and 4.195.6 kept the iOS 12.0/13.0 minimums that several Google packages, Lottie and WaterfallGrid declare. 4.208.0 raises every generated package target to at least 15.0 itself, resource bundles included. `Tuist/fix-deployment-targets.sh`, which patched the generated projects to 18.6 after each `tuist generate`, and the `PackageSettings.baseSettings` override in `Tuist/Package.swift` are removed. After pulling, run `rm -rf Tuist/.build/tuist-derived`, then `tuist install` and `tuist generate --no-open`. The app's own deployment target stays at 18.6.
- Re-resolved `Tuist/Package.resolved` with Xcode 27's SwiftPM. The committed lockfile had no `originHash`, so every `tuist install` re-resolved it and left the working tree dirty. Transitive Google packages move by patch versions only: app-check 11.3.2, GoogleDataTransport 10.1.1, GoogleUtilities 8.1.3, gtm-session-fetcher 5.3.1, nanopb 2.30910.1 and Promises 2.4.1. Direct dependencies are unchanged.
- iPad layouts no longer stretch the phone layout across the screen. The game board (single-player and multiplayer) now picks its column count for the window's shape and keeps playing-card-shaped cards: a 24-card board is 8 × 3 on a landscape iPad instead of 5 × 5 wide tiles, and no longer turns into slivers in a one-third Split View column. Small boards are capped at a sensible card size and centred. The Win, Lose, Pause, Quit and Out-of-lives cards are capped at 460pt. Settings and Achievements are capped at a 640pt reading width; the multiplayer lobby, the finished-match actions and the intro carousels' buttons at 460pt, and the carousels' subtitles at 560pt. The Levels map shows 6 tiles a row in regular width, and the menu's board grid shows 3–5 columns depending on the window's width. The multiplayer lobby now scrolls when the window is too short for it; on a landscape iPad mini it previously pushed its title and close button off the top of the screen. iPhone layouts are unchanged.
- Redrew the first-launch onboarding illustrations ("Play with Friends", "Make It Yours", "Come Back Daily", light and dark) and the review-invitation illustration (`onboarding-five-star-rating`) around Flippo, the app's card mascot, and a teal card friend, replacing the two human children they showed before. Composition, props and the soft 3D clay style are unchanged, and the art still carries no text, so no locale needs new assets. The onboarding backgrounds now match the app background colour in both appearances and each scene stays inside the top 48% that `IntroCarouselView` reserves for it; the review art remains a transparent 1200x1500 PNG. Players only see the new onboarding art. The review art appears only in the debug menu's `ReviewInvitationFullScreen` preview, because the app still does not present that view to players. Android's onboarding drawables are the same files, so both platforms change together, and the source art in `assets/onboarding/` is updated to match. No code changes.

[4.3.0] 17-09-2026

### Fixed
- Fixed archiving failing with "Unable to resolve Swift module dependency to a compatible module: 'DoMemory'" at `DoMemoryTests.swift`'s `@testable import`. Tuist was generating a second, autogenerated `DoMemory-Workspace` scheme listing every target — `DoMemoryTests` included — with `buildForArchiving = "YES"`, so archiving with it compiled the test target in Release, where `ENABLE_TESTABILITY` is off and the app module is therefore built without `-enable-testing`. The two schemes were indistinguishable in Xcode's scheme picker. A new `Workspace.swift` disables `autogeneratedWorkspaceSchemes`, leaving only the `DoMemory` scheme `Project.swift` already declared, whose build action is the app alone and whose tests run in Debug.
- Fixed multiplayer room joins being silently rejected, likely since the feature shipped in 3.0.0. `firebase/firebase-database.rules.json`'s `/multiplayerRooms/$roomId` `.read` rule only permitted a room's existing host, existing guest, or a nonexistent room to be read — but `MultiplayerService.joinRoom(code:)` reads the room before writing to it to check eligibility, and a prospective new guest is neither host nor guest yet, so that preflight read always failed and surfaced as the generic "Something went wrong" error. The rule now carries the same "room still open, no guest yet" exception `.write` already had. Found and verified via a live cross-platform match with the in-progress Android app, whose join path was unaffected because it validates against `.write` instead.
- Fixed multiplayer wins not being recorded to lifetime stats/achievements past the first win in a session. `MultiplayerRoomViewModel.handleRoomUpdate`'s `hasRecordedMultiplayerWin` latch was never cleared when a room left `.finished` (a rematch via `restartGame`/`startNewGame` puts the same room, and the same still-alive view model, back to `.playing`), unlike its sibling `hasFiredFinishHaptic` latch a few lines above, which already resets in exactly that situation with a comment explaining why. Every rematch win after the first in one multiplayer session went uncounted. Found while confirming the in-progress Android port's `MultiplayerWinGuard` (which already reset correctly) actually matched shipping iOS behavior — it didn't, so iOS now resets the same way.
- Fixed the gameplay grid briefly passing negative or non-finite card dimensions to SwiftUI while the screen transitions or an iPad split-view resize settles. Cards now remain at zero size for that transient layout pass and expand normally once geometry is available, eliminating the "Invalid frame dimension" runtime warning.
- Fixed Android's lose screen bouncing the player back to the level map's own out-of-lives modal after a retry that emptied the day's last life, instead of showing that state in place. `NavGraph.kt`'s `onRetry` for both the endless-Levels and Season routes used to `popBackStack()` whenever `GameViewModel.retry()` returned `false`; it no longer does. A new `GameUiState.livesRemaining`, read from `LevelLivesService` at loss time and re-read once the loss commits, lets the still-mounted lose overlay re-render into its out-of-lives state in place instead, matching iOS's `LoseModal`, which never dismisses itself on a failed retry.
- Fixed Android's win-screen confetti burst rendering behind the card instead of in front of it, matching iOS's `WinModal`.
- Fixed the season card's artwork on the menu answering taps a few points outside the card itself. `clipped()` and `clipShape` clip *drawing* only, not hit testing, so the image `scaledToFill` produced — marginally taller than the card it fills — kept an invisible tappable band at its top and bottom edges ever since season card artwork shipped in 4.2.0. The overflow was roughly 3pt with nothing of consequence under it, so it is unlikely to have cost anyone a tap, but it is the same defect that would have been severe on the Daily Challenge card gaining artwork in this release: that card is about four times as wide as it is tall, so 4:3 art fills it with roughly 90pt of overflow at each edge, directly over the menu's settings, create-memorama and multiplayer buttons. Both cards now mark their artwork non-interactive and declare an explicit tappable shape, so the card's rounded rectangle is exactly what answers a touch.

### Added
- Added Lottie as an SPM dependency (`airbnb/lottie-ios`, pinned 4.6.1) and a shared `LottieView` wrapper (`Modules/SharedModules/Views/`) for playing bundled Lottie JSON animations, following the same convention as `RemoteImage`.
- Flipped multiplayer to host-first on iOS: the menu's top-bar multiplayer button is now a single pull-down menu offering "Host room" or "Join room", rather than a separate icon per action. Hosting creates an empty room with no game chosen yet (`gameId == ""` on the wire — Android, which reads and writes the same `/multiplayerRooms` schema, already decodes an empty string fine). The host shares the code/QR and picks the game from inside the lobby, at any time before or after a guest joins, and can change it again right up until start. The multiplayer game picker now has its own difficulty filter, matching the All tab's `MenuDifficultyPicker`, backed by a new `MenuViewModel.multiplayerCatalog` that (unlike `memoramaArray`) is not pre-filtered to the All tab's current difficulty — custom memoramas always show regardless of the filter. This fully replaces the previous "pick a game, then create its room" flow, including the per-card "Create room" context-menu shortcut, which is now gone (see Removed, below).
- Both players now confirm readiness before a multiplayer match starts, rather than only the host having a working "Start game" button while the guest's did nothing. Either player taps "Start game" once a game is picked and both are present, which flips a new per-player `isReady` flag (`MultiplayerPlayer.isReady`, decoded as `false` when absent so a room from before this change, or from an unmodified Android client, doesn't fail to decode); the match actually starts — cards dealt, `status` to `.playing` — only once both are ready, triggered automatically from the host's client via `MultiplayerRoomViewModel.handleRoomUpdate`. Picking or changing the game resets both players' readiness, so switching games can't silently carry over a stale "ready" from before the change.
- Brought Android's multiplayer to parity with the same host-first flow: `MultiplayerService.createRoom()` now takes no board and writes an empty room (`gameId`/`gameName`/`difficulty` all `""`); a new `MultiplayerService.selectGame(roomId, board)` lets the host pick or change the game from inside the lobby through a new `MultiplayerGamePickerDialog` with its own difficulty filter (custom boards always shown), reusing `MenuScreen.kt`'s difficulty-pill visual pattern; and a new `MultiplayerPlayer.isReady` flag (decoded `false` when absent) drives the same mutual ready-check, with the host's client reactively starting the match once `MultiplayerRoom.readyToAutoStart` is true, guarded by a latch that resets whenever `status` leaves `ready` so an unrelated presence write mid-flight can't double-trigger it.
- Added typed Firebase Analytics instrumentation to Android, which previously shipped with only Firebase's auto-collected default events. A new `AnalyticsEvent` sealed class (`services/analytics/`) mirrors iOS's `enum AnalyticsEvent` case for case — same event names and parameter keys, verbatim, since both platforms log into the same Firebase project — behind a single `AnalyticsService.log` gate that is now the only sanctioned call site for `FirebaseAnalytics.logEvent`. Wired into ~20 existing screens and view models: gameplay (start/finish, card taps sampled at 20%, pause/resume, quit, retry, power-ups, lose-screen rescues, mistake-budget losses), the menu (load, difficulty, favorites, custom-memorama create/delete), Levels and Seasons (level/season start, finish, unlock, lives, skip), multiplayer (room create/join/start/finish, invites), the Daily Challenge (start, finish, streak milestones), onboarding, the notification primer, What's New, ad lifecycle (rewarded and native), and result sharing. One iOS event, `levelStarsCredited`, is deliberately not ported — Android's level/season progress services don't yet surface the improvement-only credit delta it needs — and is documented as a known gap in `AnalyticsEvent`'s class doc rather than approximated. A few other call sites are intentionally less granular than iOS (`multiplayer_room_created.game_id` is blank at creation time since Android's host-first flow creates the room before a game is picked; free play's `game_started.source` can't distinguish a board-card tap from the random-game button; a Levels/Seasons "next level" transition reports the same entry-source as a normal map visit rather than iOS's dedicated case) — each is called out in a comment at its call site in `NavGraph.kt`.

### Changed
- Updated the ReviewFlow dependency from 1.0.0 to 2.0.0. The release drops `ReviewInvitationSheet` and the modal invitation presentation entirely, leaving `ReviewInvitationFullScreen` — which now requires an `image` for its hero area and takes an `accentColor` for the gradient behind it — as the only explicit invitation view. Nothing player-facing changes: the app has never adopted ReviewFlow's own invitation view, and `AppReviews`' `ReviewManager`, the eligibility policy and the migrated win counts are untouched by the major bump. The only call site was the simulator-only debug menu's preview row, which now presents `ReviewInvitationFullScreen` in a `.fullScreenCover` with a new transparent-background `onboarding-five-star-rating` illustration and `Color.primaryColor`.
- The first-launch onboarding carousel's three slides ("Play with Friends", "Make It Yours", "Come Back Daily") now each show a full-width illustration, with light and dark variants, in place of the small tinted symbol. `IntroSlide` gained an optional `illustration` asset name; slides without one, including all of the Levels intro, keep the symbol layout unchanged. The artwork fades into the app background at its edges and sits centred with the text, and on iPad it is width-capped with side fades so the text never drops under the page dots. Source art lives in `assets/onboarding/`.
- Redesigned the All tab's memorama card on both platforms. The favorite (heart) toggle grew from a 15pt/18sp glyph with no real hit area into a 20pt/20dp heart in a 36dp tinted circle with a full 44pt (iOS) / 48dp (Android) tap target, and now carries an accessibility label (`menu_favorite_add`/`menu_favorite_remove`, added to all ten iOS locales; Android's equivalent strings already existed but were unused until now). The card's per-board name label was removed — the catalog's `name` field is just the board's own emoji, so the label was pure repetition — and the board emoji grew from 44pt to 52pt on iOS to fill the freed space. On iOS, the card's separate "Create room" multiplayer button was also removed; multiplayer moved off the card entirely in this same release (see Added/Removed, below) in favor of a host-first flow that starts from the menu's top bar. Android's card never had a per-card multiplayer button, so nothing changed there.
- The post-game win screen now plays a one-shot confetti burst behind the card as it appears, replacing the previous static decorative circles. On a Levels or Season win, the 3-star row pops in one star at a time (roughly 180ms apart) via a small Lottie animation per star instead of appearing all at once as static icons; stars beyond what was earned stay dim and static, unchanged. Both animations are hand-authored JSON, since no After Effects export pipeline was available, and both respect `accessibilityReduceMotion` — motion-sensitive players see the modal in its final static state immediately, with no animation.
- Six more moments now play a small Lottie effect instead of a static change, all sharing the win screen's hand-authored-JSON, `accessibilityReduceMotion`-respecting approach: the lose modal's hero glyph cracks like a clock on a timeout or shakes with an x on a mistake-budget loss; a lost heart on the Levels or Season map header breaks, and a refilled one fills back in; the timer chip's ice shatters the moment a Freeze effect runs out, a moment easy to miss without it; and the header's star count sparkles on a credit, never on a spend. Every clip is now shared with the in-progress Android port from one JSON file per animation at the repository root, so the two apps cannot drift on frame count, colour or timing.
- The All tab's read-only difficulty badge is now a tappable `MenuDifficultyPicker`, matching the in-progress Android port's catalog picker. Choosing a difficulty there filters the catalog immediately, updates the player's stored setting used by later games and Settings, and logs the same `difficulty_selected` analytics event the onboarding picker already fires — replacing the previous behaviour of sending players to Settings to change it.
- Brought Android's lose screen to visual and behavioral parity with iOS's `LoseModal`: a reason pill (icon + "You lose" / "Too many mistakes"), the `LivesRow` with its lost-heart break animation and out-of-lives messaging, matching button styles, order and gating for the ad/star rescues (watch-ad-for-life or watch-ad-to-forgive leads, then buy-life-or-forgive-with-stars, then Try Again, then skip-with-stars, then Go to menu), and the skip-level confirmation dialog's filled amber confirm / outlined cancel styling.
- The menu's Daily Challenge card now carries bundled artwork — a calendar page with a streak flame in the brand indigo and orange — in place of the flat indigo fill it has had since the feature shipped, so it reads as a pair with the season card beside it rather than as the unfinished half of the row. Both the full-width card and the compact one that shares a row with a running season draw the same image behind a leading-to-trailing scrim in the card's own accent colour, which keeps the unshadowed white title, subtitle and streak badge legible; the full-width layout takes a heavier scrim, because being roughly four times as wide as it is tall it crops a narrow band from 4:3 art and scales the motif up underneath the text. The art is bundled rather than fetched from Firebase because, unlike a season's, it is fixed and ships with the app — the reason `RemoteImageService` keeps season art out of the binary does not apply. It is decorative, so it is hidden from VoiceOver rather than having its asset name read out after the card's title.
- Brought Android's first-launch onboarding to parity with iOS. It was a static three-page text screen whose copy (match the pairs / play at your pace / make it yours) described the rules rather than the features and corresponded to no iOS slide; it now shows the same three illustrated slides — "Play with Friends", "Make It Yours", "Come Back Daily" — with swipe paging, dot indicators and the same Skip / Get Started chrome. The `intro_*` strings were already translated into all ten locales for the Levels intro, so nothing needed new translation, and the nine now-unused `onboarding_*` strings were removed from every locale. The chrome moved into a shared `ui/components/IntroCarousel.kt` that both intros use, mirroring how iOS's `FeatureIntroView` and `LevelsIntroView` share `IntroCarouselView`. The artwork is selected from a new `Palette.isDark` rather than a `-night` resource qualifier, because the app has its own `ThemePreference` and a qualifier follows the *system* appearance — a player forcing light mode on a dark phone would otherwise have seen dark artwork on a light background.
- Android's Daily Challenge card now carries the same bundled calendar-and-streak-flame artwork as iOS, in both the compact and the full-width layout, in place of the flat accent fill. `CompactCardLayout` gained a bundled-drawable path beside its existing Firebase one: a season's art is authored per season after the build and must be fetched, while the Daily Challenge's is fixed and ships in the APK. One image serves both appearances, since the scrim is the appearance-aware accent colour. iOS's hit-testing defect has no counterpart here — `matchParentSize` gives the image exactly the card's measured size and Compose bounds pointer input to the layout node — which was confirmed by tapping the header buttons over the full-width card rather than assumed.

### Removed
- Removed the difficulty-picker screen from first-launch onboarding on both platforms. With Levels as the landing tab and the All tab's difficulty badge now tappable (see above), asking a new player to pick a difficulty before they had seen the game added a step without adding a choice they couldn't make later. On iOS, `HomeView` now finishes onboarding right after the feature-intro carousel, creating the player's `UserSettings` record with `.medium` as the default difficulty instead of waiting for a tap. On Android, `OnboardingViewModel` no longer has a `DIFFICULTY` step — its intro carousel's last "Continue" (now "Get started") and its "Skip" both complete onboarding directly, persisting `Difficulty.MEDIUM` via `UserPreferences.completeOnboarding`.
- Removed the All tab card's per-card "Create room" context-menu action on iOS, now that hosting a room starts empty from the menu's top bar instead of from a specific board (see Added, above). The card's context menu now shows nothing at all for a catalog board — Delete, its only other item, is custom-memorama-only — and no longer attaches an (otherwise empty) context menu to those cards.
- Removed the Levels intro carousel's automatic first-time presentation on iOS. It no longer opens on a player's first visit to the Levels tab and no longer needs the launch-sequence gating (`MenuView`'s `launchSequenceFinished`/`canPresentLevelsIntro` and the `isLaunchSurfacePresented` plumbing from `ContentView`) that kept it from racing the ATT prompt, What's New sheet and notification primer. It now opens only from the info ("?") button in the Levels map header, which already bypassed the gate. The now-unused `LevelsIntroGate` "seen" tracker, its `levelsIntroShown` UserDefaults key, and its tests were removed along with it.
- Removed the same automatic first-time presentation of the Levels intro on Android, which had still been firing from `LevelsViewModel`'s initialiser. The map header's "?" button is now the only way in on both platforms. `LevelsIntroGate` is still written on dismissal so the "seen it" flag stays truthful for anything that wants it later.

[4.2.0] 10-09-2026

### Added
- Added Season Levels, a limited-time themed progression mode. A season — "Spooky Season", "Christmas Season" — is published from the Firebase Realtime Database `/seasons` node with its own emoji pool, length, icon, accent colour and localized title, and appears in the app only while its date window is open. Its levels unlock in order and are rated 1-3 stars on the same thresholds endless Levels uses, so a 3-star board means the same thing in both modes.
- Added a season card to the menu, sharing a row with the Daily Challenge while a season is running and showing the season's progress through its levels. With no active season the Daily Challenge card keeps the full-width layout it has always had.
- Added a season level map with the season's countdown, a progress bar, and a completion state once every level is cleared, so a finished season has somewhere to land instead of ending on a wall of cleared tiles.
- Added Firebase-supplied artwork to seasons: a full-bleed background behind the season's level map and an image on the season's menu card, published as `backgroundImageURL` and `cardImageURL` on the season's `/seasons` node. A season may also carry `backgroundImageURLDark`, used instead of the main background in dark mode — level tiles are near-white in light mode and near-black in dark, so artwork that separates from them in one appearance collapses into them in the other. A season with only one image uses it for both. Both are optional and remote rather than bundled, so a season published after a player's last app update still arrives with its own art instead of showing the previous season's picture. A season with no artwork, a blank URL, an unreachable image or no network renders exactly as it did before — the flat app background behind the map, the flat accent colour on the card.
- Added `Scripts/upload_seasons.py` and the seeded Spooky Season 2026 catalog it publishes, so a season can be put live or pulled without an app release. It checks a season against the same rules the app applies before decoding one — the 12-distinct-emoji floor, the date window, a usable English title — so a season that would be silently skipped on device is rejected before it is uploaded. Setting a season's `enabled` to `false` is the kill switch: the season leaves the app on the next menu load.
- Added a `season_levels_entered` analytics event, fired every time a season's level map appears — including returning to it from a level — carrying the season's ID. The existing `screenView` event fires alongside it but carries no season identity, so a season's own entry funnel can now be filtered by `season_id` in Firebase.

### Fixed
- Fixed season titles falling back to English for Spanish and Chinese players. A season's localized strings are keyed by locale, and the lookup only ever shortens the device's identifier — so an `es-419` key was reachable only from a device reporting exactly `es_419`, never from `es_MX`, `es_AR` or `es_ES`, and a `zh-Hans` key was unreachable from any mainland device because iOS canonicalizes `zh_Hans_CN` to `zh_CN` and drops the script. The published catalog now uses keys the lookup can reach, and a test pins every supported locale against the identifiers iOS actually produces so a future season cannot reintroduce it. Because the fix is in the catalog rather than the app, it reaches players who have not updated.

### Changed
- The level map's current-level tile now pulses in place — an expanding ring plus a subtle breathing scale — and every tile presses down and springs back on tap, so the map invites a tap instead of sitting static. Locked and cleared tiles pay nothing for it; only the current tile animates. The season progress bar's fill now eases to its new width with a spring instead of snapping instantly when a level is cleared. Both Levels and Seasons share the level map, so both get the change.
- The season level map's navigation bar is transparent when the season carries artwork, so the image reaches the top of the screen instead of stopping at a band of flat colour. A season without artwork keeps the opaque bar.
- Spooky Season now runs 30 levels rather than 20, and carries titles in all ten supported languages. Extending a running season needs no migration: completion is stored as the level after the last one, so a player who had finished all 20 simply finds level 21 unlocked and their progress reading 20 of 30.
- Season artwork is cached in memory and on disk after its first fetch, so it costs nothing on later launches and does not flash back to the flat accent colour each time the menu's tab bar rebuilds the season card. Replacing the art at the same URL still reaches players once the stored response expires.
- Moved Levels to the first tab on the menu and All to the last, so the tab order now reads Levels, My memoramas, All. Levels is also the tab the menu opens on, making the progression mode the landing screen rather than the full game grid.
- The Levels intro now waits for the launch sequence to finish before it presents. With Levels as the landing tab its cover would otherwise open while the ATT prompt, the What's New sheet or the notification permission primer still owned the screen — the same collision each of those already guards against. It still shows once per install, still reopens from "How Levels works", and holds off the app-open ad for as long as it is up.
- Seasons draw on the same daily lives budget and the same star wallet as endless Levels, rather than granting a second set of free plays. Season stars are deliberately kept out of `levels.lifetimeStars`, which stays the endless-Levels mastery score.
- Season progress is stored per season, so a season ending cannot disturb endless-Levels progress and a season that returns next year resumes where it left off.
- Extracted the level map — the tile grid, its styling and its tap rules — out of the Levels screen so both modes render the same map under their own headers. Endless Levels is unchanged.
- A season whose emoji pool holds fewer than 12 distinct entries is rejected when it is read, because the difficulty curve tops out at 12 pairs and a smaller pool cannot fill its own later boards. The floor is derived from the curve, so retuning the curve moves it. Every other failure — no network, no cached season, the kill switch off, a malformed accent colour — falls back rather than showing a broken season.
- The season screens read in all ten supported languages rather than falling back to English, and the progress bar now announces "7 of 20 levels cleared" to VoiceOver instead of the bare "7 / 20".
- Refreshed the in-app What's New sheet for this release — Season Levels, Themed Artwork, Tap to Play, and Season Progress — replacing the stale 4.0.0-era Levels/Star Ratings/Power-Ups/Lives & Mistakes content, and localized the eight new strings into all 10 supported locales. Uploaded matching localized "What's New" and Promotional Text copy to App Store Connect separately. Bumped `MARKETING_VERSION` to 4.2.0 in `Project.swift` and regenerated `project.pbxproj`.

[4.1.0] 01-09-2026

### Added
- Added a notification permission primer (NotificationPermissionKit) that explains what reminders are for before the one-time iOS authorization alert, shown once per install on the menu and reused by the Settings toggle.
- Added a "Rate DoMemory" row in Settings linking to the App Store review page, so leaving a review no longer depends on catching the throttled system prompt.
- Added a "What's New" row in Settings that presents the current release highlights outside the automatic version gate.

### Fixed
- Fixed the Settings screen having no scroll view, which clipped every row past the viewport. "Restore purchases" was completely unreachable, leaving reinstalling customers with no way to recover their Remove Ads entitlement.
- Fixed the Settings header rendering underneath the status bar and Dynamic Island.
- Fixed scrolled content passing under the floating back button on Settings and Achievements, by giving both navigation bars an opaque background instead of leaving them transparent.
- Fixed Settings row subtitles truncating mid-word, by replacing the per-row action pills with chevrons and switches where the pill was not the point.
- Fixed notification permission granted from the menu never enabling reminders: the grant did not set `notificationsEnabled`, which every scheduling method checks, so users who accepted received no reminders until they separately toggled Settings on.
- Fixed the ATT, ads, and notification prompts firing back to back on first menu appearance with no context between them.
- Fixed the What's New sheet appearing on a brand-new install, greeting a first-time player with release notes for a version they had never not had. Nothing wrote `whatsNewLastSeenVersion` on install, so the version gate read the empty slot as an upgrade. A first launch now records the running version and stays quiet. Onboarding state, not the empty slot, decides this — someone upgrading from a build that predates the What's New manager also has nothing stored, and they still see the sheet.
